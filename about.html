About the studio

Clay finds its shape twice.
Once on the wheel, once in the fire.

I'm Mara Voss, and SMBstudio is the one-room studio I've worked out of since 2013, tucked into the ground floor of an old textile mill on the edge of Asheville. Eleven years in, the wheel still surprises me some mornings and fights me on others.

I throw in a dense stoneware body sourced from a quarry two counties over, chosen for the way it takes a glaze without losing its color under the flame. Every mug, bowl, and vase that leaves this studio has been centered, thrown, trimmed, bisque fired, glazed, and fired again by hand, no molds, no shortcuts.

The process

From a lump of clay to a finished piece, six stages, roughly three weeks.

01

Wedge & Center

Clay is kneaded to press out air, then centered dead true on the wheel head before a single wall goes up.

02

Throw

The form is pulled up in a few steady passes. Most pieces take under ten minutes on the wheel itself.

03

Trim & Dry

Once leather hard, the foot is trimmed clean. Pieces rest under cloth for five to seven days to dry evenly.

04

Bisque Fire

A slow first firing to 1,830°F hardens the clay body enough to handle glaze without dissolving.

05

Glaze

Glazes are mixed in small batches in the studio and applied by dipping, pouring, or brush, piece by piece.

06

Final Fire

A final firing to cone 6, around 2,232°F, melts the glaze into glass and sets the color for good.
